What our checks found

  • Nature is perfectly balanced, 120 messages each for verbal, physical, social and cyber. That is a sign the data is synthetic, and we say so.
  • “Minimal” means a question, not an incident. It is a different skill from judging how serious bullying is.
  • The datasets' own language labels mean different things in different files, so we tag language by content instead.
  • 10 messages share a near-duplicate group with another message. They are kept on the same side of the split.
  • Slur terms are masked as placeholders in the datasets, so they stay out of prompts.

The locked test set

We locked one third of the data as a test set: 160 messages (16 Minimal, 39 Limited, 88 High, 17 Emergency), grouped so near-duplicates stay together and stratified by risk, with a fixed random seed. Rules and settings were chosen on the other 320 messages only. One disclosure: we looked at the test results once before adding the final system, and the notebook says so.

Slides 5 and 9: methods and baselines

Five systems, simplest first

Each step adds something and costs something. The plain model catches 10 of 17 emergencies. The safety hybrid catches 16.

  1. Always guess High. The floor: what a system with no understanding scores.
  2. Keyword rules. What a checklist written by a teacher would do. No training.
  3. TF-IDF with logistic regression. The standard baseline. Character n-grams cope with Kreol spelling.
  4. Plus an Emergency rule. If a threat, weapon or self-harm pattern appears, force Emergency.
  5. Plus a lowered alarm threshold (final). Also flag Emergency when the model is only fairly sure. Chosen on development data to reach at least 95% recall.

Slide 9: safety trade-off

Catching more emergencies means more false alarms

A counsellor can dismiss a false alarm. A missed emergency cannot be undone. So we accept the extra alarms.

Emergency recall against false-alarm rate

Hover the line to read any threshold

We fixed the rule in advance: pick the largest threshold whose cross-validated recall on development data reaches 95%. That gave t = 0.19. The locked test set was not used to choose it. On the test set the final system caught 16 of 17 emergencies and raised 14 false alarms. All 14 were truly High cases, so a counsellor would have wanted to read them anyway.

Reading the mistakes
  • Under-triage (5). All are indirect harm: exclusion, a group made to mock someone, an edited photo, a rumour. Some of our labels are arguable, which is why a second person labelling matters.
  • False alarms (14). All truly High, pushed up by the safety setting. Cheap, but they would flood a real queue.
  • Question vs incident (4). When unsure, the bot should ask a clarifying question.

The one missed emergency

Content note: the next message is a synthetic example about not wanting to live.

“Mo nepli anvi viv akoz bann posts ek stickers zot pe fer lor mwa.”
Kreol. In English: I no longer want to live because of the posts and stickers they make about me. Our label Emergency · model said High

No rule and no training example covered this Kreol phrase. A Kreol self-harm word list, built with the school, is the most valuable fix. We chose not to add it and re-score the same test set, because that would be training on the test set. It goes into the next round and is tested on fresh messages.
